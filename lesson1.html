What is Phishing?

Core concept & how attacks work

Phishing is a social engineering attack where criminals impersonate legitimate organisations via email, SMS, or fake websites to steal sensitive information — passwords, credit card numbers, or install malware on your device.

The term comes from "fishing" — casting a wide bait hoping someone bites. Modern phishing uses personalised spear-phishing targeting specific individuals or companies.

🚨 Real Incident: 2023 Healthcare Breach

A major healthcare provider lost $7.5 million after an employee clicked a phishing invoice link. Attackers gained system access within 90 minutes and exfiltrated patient records for 3 months before discovery.

Red Flag Checklist

Hover each sign to learn more

  • 🚨
    Urgent / threatening language "Your account will be suspended TODAY!" — creates panic to bypass logic
  • 🔗
    Mismatched sender / URL "paypa1.com" vs "paypal.com" — one character off
  • 👤
    Generic salutation "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name
  • ✏️
    Grammar & spelling errors Legitimate companies proofread; scammers often don't
  • 📎
    Unexpected attachments Fake invoices, receipts, or "important documents"
  • 🔑
    Requests for credentials No legitimate service emails you asking for your password

Real-World Phishing Scenarios

Learn from actual attack patterns — spot the red flags

🎣 Email Phishing

The Fake Bank Alert

You receive an email from "security@citibank-alert.com" saying "Unusual login detected from Moscow. Verify your identity within 24 hours or your account will be locked." There's a blue "Secure Verify" button inside.

🔴 Domain: citibank-alert.com (not citibank.com) 🔴 Urgent 24-hour deadline 🔴 Suspicious geographic detail ✅ What to do: Log in via citibank.com directly
📱 SMS Smishing

Fake Parcel Delivery

You get an SMS: "Royal Mail: Your parcel is held. Pay £1.99 customs fee to release it. Click: royalmail-delivery.info/pay" — Even if you're expecting a package, this is a trap.

🔴 Domain: royalmail-delivery.info (not royalmail.com) 🔴 Small fee to steal card details 🔴 SMS from unknown number ✅ Check via official Royal Mail tracking portal
🎁 Prize Phishing

The Prize Winner Trap

An email arrives: "Congratulations! You've been selected for a £500 Amazon Gift Card. You're the 1,000,000th visitor! Claim your reward now — expires in 1 hour!" Complete with a spinning wheel graphic.

🔴 "1,000,000th visitor" is always fake 🔴 Artificial scarcity (1-hour expiry) 🔴 Unsolicited reward you never entered ✅ Delete immediately, never claim unsolicited prizes
🏢 Spear Phishing

The CEO Impersonation

Your company's "CEO" emails from ceo@company-corp.net asking you to urgently wire £15,000 to a supplier account for a confidential deal. They ask you to keep it quiet from your manager.

🔴 Domain: company-corp.net vs company.com 🔴 "Keep it quiet" — secrecy request 🔴 Urgency around money transfer ✅ Always verify wire requests via phone using known numbers

Glossary of Terms

Key concepts explained — click to expand

Phishing+
A cyberattack where criminals send fraudulent messages impersonating legitimate organisations to trick users into revealing sensitive information like passwords or credit card numbers.
Spear Phishing+
A targeted form of phishing directed at a specific individual or organisation. Attackers research their target using LinkedIn, company websites, or social media to craft convincing personalised messages.
Smishing (SMS Phishing)+
Phishing conducted via text message / SMS. Often disguised as parcel delivery notifications, bank alerts, or prize winnings with malicious links embedded.
Vishing (Voice Phishing)+
Phone-based phishing where attackers call victims pretending to be tech support, bank agents, or government officials to extract sensitive information verbally.
URL Spoofing / Typosquatting+
Registering domains that closely mimic real ones — e.g. "paypa1.com" or "arnazon.com" — to deceive users who don't carefully read the address bar.
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)+
A security layer requiring two forms of verification: something you know (password) and something you have (a code sent to your phone or an authenticator app). Massively reduces account takeover risk.
Social Engineering+
Psychological manipulation of people into divulging confidential information. Phishing is the most common form, but social engineering also includes pretexting, baiting, and quid pro quo attacks.
