What is Social Engineering?

Psychological manipulation, not technical hacking

Social engineering is the art of manipulating people into breaking standard security procedures or revealing confidential information. Attackers exploit trust, fear, curiosity, and urgency — not software vulnerabilities.

Instead of hacking your password, they hack you into giving it away voluntarily. The most sophisticated security systems mean nothing if a human can be tricked.

📰 Parry Security Case Study

An employee received a call from "the CEO" using AI voice cloning, asking to transfer $50,000 urgently. The employee followed protocol — called back on the official number — and stopped the fraud. Always verify through a separate channel.

Common Attack Tactics

How attackers manipulate you

  • 🎭
    Impersonation / Pretexting Fake IT support, bank officials, government agents — creating a fake scenario to extract info
  • 👥
    Quid Pro Quo "I'll fix your computer, just give me remote access" — offering a fake service for access
  • 📞
    Vishing (Voice Phishing) Phone calls pretending to be from banks, police, or tech support to steal OTPs
  • 📱
    Smishing (SMS Phishing) Fake parcel delivery texts, bank alerts, or prize notifications via SMS
  • 🎣
    Baiting Leaving infected USB drives in parking lots — curiosity leads to infection
  • 🚪
    Tailgating / Piggybacking Following someone into a secure area by pretending to have forgotten a badge

How to Defend Against Social Engineering

The Parry Security "Verify First" Protocol

🛑
STOP – Don't act on urgency Scammers create panic to bypass your logic
🤔
THINK – Does this make sense? Would a bank call asking for OTP? No.
📞
VERIFY – Use a separate channel Call back using a known official number
🚨
REPORT – Tell your security team Reporting helps protect everyone
🔐
Never share OTPs or passwords No legitimate entity will ask for them
👥
Establish verification codes Create a family/business code word

Glossary of Terms

Key social engineering concepts — click to expand

Social Engineering+
Psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. Exploits trust, fear, and urgency rather than technical vulnerabilities.
Pretexting+
Creating a fabricated scenario (pretext) to trick someone into revealing information. Often involves impersonating authority figures like IT support or bank managers.
Vishing (Voice Phishing)+
Phone-based social engineering where attackers call victims pretending to be legitimate organisations to extract sensitive information like OTPs or card numbers.
Smishing (SMS Phishing)+
Phishing conducted via text message. Common examples include fake parcel delivery alerts, bank fraud warnings, or prize scams with malicious links.
Quid Pro Quo+
"Something for something" — attackers offer a fake service (like tech support) in exchange for sensitive information or access.
AI Voice Cloning+
Using artificial intelligence to replicate someone's voice from short audio samples, making impersonation attacks extremely convincing.
Tailgating / Piggybacking+
Physical social engineering where an unauthorised person follows an authorised person into a secure area, often by claiming to have forgotten their badge.
