Social Media Risks

How oversharing makes you vulnerable

  • 📍
    Real-time Location Sharing Posting vacation photos while away tells burglars your home is empty
  • 🎂
    Personal Information Exposure Birth date, pet names, mother's maiden name — all answers to security questions
  • 📸
    Photo Metadata Photos often contain GPS coordinates and device information
  • 🎭
    Impersonation & Catfishing Scraped photos and info used to create fake profiles
  • 📧
    Data Aggregation Companies collect and sell your data to advertisers and data brokers
  • 🔓
    Job & Reputation Damage Employers check social media — inappropriate posts can cost opportunities
✅

Data Privacy Best Practices

Take control of your digital footprint

  • 🔒
    Use Strong Privacy Settings Make profiles private — only friends can see your posts
  • ⏰
    Share Vacation Photos AFTER Returning Post memories when you're back home, not during the trip
  • 🔍
    Google Yourself Regularly See what information about you is publicly accessible
  • 📋
    Limit What You Share Don't post your full birth date, address, phone number, or ID documents
  • 🔐
    Use Different Emails for Different Services Makes it harder to track you across platforms
  • 🗑️
    Delete Old Accounts Inactive accounts still hold your personal data

Social Media Safety Rules

Think before you post — protect your digital reputation

⏰
The "Holiday Rule" Post vacation photos AFTER you return home, not during
🔒
Privacy Settings First Set profiles to private before posting anything personal
👥
Don't Accept Strangers Scammers create fake profiles to gather your information
🔐
Never Share Security Answers Mother's maiden name, first pet, school names — keep them private
📱
Log Out of Shared Devices Always log out from public computers or friends' phones
🗑️
Delete Old Posts Old content can resurface — regularly audit your posts

Glossary of Terms

Key concepts explained — click to expand

Digital Footprint+
The trail of data you leave online — posts, comments, likes, searches, and check-ins. Once online, it's nearly impossible to fully delete.
Data Broker+
Companies that collect, aggregate, and sell your personal information. They know your address, income, shopping habits, and more.
Metadata+
Data about your data — photos contain GPS coordinates, timestamps, device information. Tools can strip this before sharing.
Social Engineering+
Psychological manipulation to extract information. Oversharing on social media provides attackers with valuable ammunition.
Impersonation Attack+
Attackers create fake profiles using your publicly available photos and information to trick your friends or damage your reputation.
Doxxing+
Publishing someone's private information (address, phone number, workplace) online maliciously, often after gathering from public social media.
